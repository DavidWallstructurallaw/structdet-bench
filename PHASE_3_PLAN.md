## 2. Objective and bounded capability activation

### 2.1 Required working result

Given an explicitly recorded longitudinal study, produce an offline report that can answer:

1. What structural classes were observed at each declared state and pipeline stage, under which sampling protocol and evidence policy?
2. Which observed classes were retained, became unobserved, or reappeared across compatible measurements, including the protected tail?
3. What does the categorical null predict under its stated assumptions, and what measured diversity-decay summary is supported by a separately documented trajectory?
4. Which reference classes meet or fall below a declared generation-probability threshold within a finite tested intervention family?
5. Which members of a defensibly established pre-intervention missing set are expressed after an independently grounded recovery intervention, with appropriate validation and no direct answer substitution?
6. What changes reflect the evaluated lineage, and what changes reflect measurement, classification, or benchmark revision?

The new flow is:

`passive local records -> versioned lineage/trajectory binding -> existing evidence and M populations -> observed support and stage accounting -> explicit null/empirical/assay calculations -> scoped recovery -> versioned report and replay manifest`.

## 3. Longitudinal objects, identities, and comparison semantics

### 3.1 State, stage, and event vocabulary

A model generation, a corpus snapshot, an evaluation run, and a recursive transition are different units.

| Object | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| `state_id` | An explicitly identified model/system or corpus state. It names a subject, not a fresh observation by itself. |
| `state_kind` | `model_system`, `corpus`, or `categorical_scenario`; kinds cannot be silently pooled. |
| `model_state_ref` | Parameter/checkpoint identity and known/unavailable state, plus serving, retrieval, tool and system configuration where relevant. |
| `stage_role` | `probe_output`, `generated_candidates`, `post_preprocessing`, `post_selection`, or `training_exposure`; model-probe and corpus-stage statistics remain distinct. |
| `measurement_ref` | A cell and its selected observations, population view, schema, protocol, budget, evidence and consumed-snapshot identity. |
| `transition_id` | A documented reproduction/selection/learning/configuration event with parents, child, kind, evidence and actual effects. |
| `recursive_round_index` | A nonnegative integer on a named trajectory, with supported transition distance. Filenames, checkpoint names, timestamps, and API-call counts cannot supply it. |
| `trajectory_id` | A preregistered ordered path through named states and transitions, including planned observation positions and known gaps. |
| `probe_panel_id` | A recurring task/prompt/protocol stratum at the same resolution, measured across states. It is not a training replicate. |
| `lineage_root_unit_id` | A registered whole-lineage sampling/sensitivity unit with known dependency group and uncertainty about ancestry. |
| `intervention_id` | A declared change in evidence, tools, retrieval, curation or model state, with pre/post targets and evidence. |
| `assay_id` | A fixed-state, finite-family probability-threshold assessment; an assay does not create a training generation. |
| `revision_id` | A measurement/schema/evidence correction. Reanalysis remains distinct from new sampling or a model update. |

A corpus can have observed structural breadth without showing that its downstream model can express those structures. Training-exposure counts, source-document counts, token weights, probe trials, and accepted labels remain separately named.

### 3.2 Directed lineage and incomplete history

Reproduction edges have explicit types: categorical reconstruction, synthetic replacement, accumulation, subset reuse, curation, learning update, inference/configuration intervention, or a declared composite with individually recorded stages. A fixed-model inference intervention does not increment the recursive learning/reproduction clock.

Known forward reproduction edges must be acyclic and consistent with their declared temporal order. A fork or multiple parents is allowed. Unknown ancestors remain unknown; no-parent metadata is not evidence of an independent root. A dangling or contradictory edge restricts the affected trajectory and its descendants, while unrelated supported states remain usable.

The general evidence graph may contain later reviews, corrections and cross-references. These do not inherit a universal DAG rule. Typed reproduction cycles and historical review links must not be conflated.

A documented update may leave parameters or outputs unchanged. Record the attempted process, actual completed transition and evidenced effective change separately. Identical checkpoint bytes alone neither establish a reproduction event nor erase an otherwise documented event.

Every trajectory records its planned observation schedule. A planned but missing observation remains missing. An intentionally unsampled interval may have a known recursive distance if all intervening transitions and their relevant regimes are documented. It is never compressed into one step. No interpolation, forward fill, retroactive zero assignment, automatic branch selection, or backfilling from another chain is allowed.

Multiple-parent transitions expose the supplied contribution units and weights. Identical hashes, different source names, or token proportions alone do not establish independent structural contributions. Descendant outcomes cannot be causally assigned to one parent by matching a label.

### 3.3 Compatibility and budget matching

Comparable measurements identify task context C, consequence horizon T, analytic resolution, structural schema and rubric, registry, actual elicitation protocol, population view, material role, output-selection rule, and sample budget. A model-state change is the object of a longitudinal study and is explicitly recorded; unrelated protocol, taxonomy or validation changes are confounders rather than implicit controls.

The initial profile supports the existing sorting identities and eight registered classes. It permits new state and evidence records without changing that taxonomy. A newly encountered mechanism is preserved as an anomaly/candidate under the existing admission rules.

For matched observed-probe trajectories, declare one fixed realization budget per panel before inspecting results and retain its first requested positions. Each position is selected before classification or validity filtering. A missing position cannot be replaced by a later success. Phase 2's 20-realization, six-block A/B/C design is not a universal recursive-study budget.

Descriptive unmatched inventories may remain visible with their own counts. A cross-state scalar intended to represent matched support change needs compatible framing, protocol and selection; changing sample size, classification coverage or valid-only conditioning is disclosed and can withhold the matched result. The categorical resampling size n, probe sample size N, token budget, and number of training rows remain distinct.

### 3.4 Per-state numerical and evidence rules

Reuse M admission and the `classified_all` and `classified_valid` populations. Carry all five coverage ratios, unclassified/provisional/conflicted records, validity states, source identities and missingness. Do not infer labels or turn a validator timeout into class absence.

Add `gini_simpson_diversity = 1 - SCI` using the same empirical distribution. Compute the complement from the exact SCI ratio where available. This definition changes neither the existing SCI nor Q: the without-replacement structural pair statistic Q is a separate Phase 2 quantity.

A known nonempty population containing one class has diversity zero. A known empty classified population has observed-support size zero but no probability distribution; its diversity is `undefined`, not one. An unresolved common frame or selected population makes the affected quantity `unavailable`.

Primary SHL fitting in this first profile uses matched `classified_all` probes with complete hard-class coverage at the prescribed selected budget. This is a new conservative engineering gate, not a source-specified universal percentage. A valid-only sensitivity fit additionally requires complete decisive-validity records and supported classification of every established-valid output; its varying valid denominator is shown explicitly. It is a fit to a conditioned population, not a matched unconditional generation law. Supported per-state M summaries remain available when these stronger fitting gates fail.

Phase 2's P1/P5 quality thresholds are not imported as universal SHL or recovery thresholds. Each new endpoint uses its stated requirements.

### 3.5 Observed retention, loss, recurrence, and protected tails

For two compatible observed sets A and B at named endpoints:

- Retained classes: `A intersect B`.
- Became unobserved: `A minus B`.
- Newly observed at the second endpoint: `B minus A`.
- `observed_retention_fraction = |A intersect B| / |A|` when A is known and nonempty.
- `observed_loss_fraction = |A minus B| / |A|` under the same condition.

These are project-defined descriptive set summaries. They retain the occurrence-based or empirical-frequency-thresholded support type, budget, view and scope. An empty known A gives an undefined retention/loss fraction; an unknown A gives unavailability. A shrinking count can hide simultaneous loss and gain, so set identities accompany size changes.

A class observed again after an earlier nonappearance is labeled `observed_reappearance`, with intervening gaps visible. It is not automatically external recovery or reversal of representational loss. Absorbing-zero rules belong only to exact categorical scenarios.

Protected tail membership is preregistered through a pinned registry and rationale, not selected after seeing which classes failed. Keep each designated class's counts, frequencies, observation state and review availability by time. High consequence and low frequency are separate designations. Tail-reference preservation cannot add class mass to probe samples or force rare examples into a frozen prefix.

Stage-retention tables may compare candidate, preprocessing, selection and exposure sets only when they share the declared class meanings and linked material scope. Their denominators identify the upstream stage. No scalar from these tables is named universal `structural_transmission`; no missing downstream causal mapping is inferred.

## 4. Categorical-null and transition-accounting contract

### 4.1 Exact source model

**Source-derived: SI sections 5.1-5.6 and Appendix A; EC section 4.** For a finite registered class space, supplied probability vector p_t, integer n, and closed multinomial reconstruction:

\[
X_t\mid p_t\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,p_t),\qquad p_{t+1}=X_t/n.
\]

No new class, external correction, mutation, retained-data contribution, or additional selection operation enters this particular transition. Under these assumptions:

\[
\operatorname{supp}(p_{t+1})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(p_t),
\]

\[
D_t=1-\sum_z p_{t,z}^2,\quad
\mathbb E[D_{t+1}\mid p_t]=(1-1/n)D_t,\quad
\mathbb E[D_t]=(1-1/n)^tD_0,
\]

\[
\Pr(p_{t+1,z}=0\mid p_t)=(1-p_{t,z})^n,
\]

\[
L_t=\lVert p_t-q\rVert_2^2,\qquad
\mathbb E[L_{t+1}\mid p_t]=L_t+D_t/n.
\]

The target q must be supplied, normalized and defined on the same state space. It is not defaulted to the initial distribution or a uniform distribution. The null's q is distinct from an independently evidenced deployment distribution or EC's omitted deployment mass.

### 4.2 Implementation and edge behavior

The new `categorical_null.py` evaluates these formulas on explicit finite scenarios and checks supplied count transitions. It does not train a model, draw a production synthetic corpus, or infer that an empirical pipeline follows the null. Exhaustive tiny-state enumeration is permitted in trusted tests only.

Inputs require exact rational or bounded finite-decimal probabilities, nonnegative entries, exact normalization, class IDs, n, requested transition count and an assumption record. No smoothing may introduce mass in an absent class. Invalid probability totals remain errors rather than being silently renormalized.

An actual supplied child count vector must sum to its declared n. A child assigning positive mass to a parent zero is inconsistent with the specified closed scenario. Record that scenario violation without deleting the observation or imposing the same constraint on an empirical neural trajectory.

Pathwise support non-expansion applies to positive-probability support in the exact scenario. It does not imply monotonic empirical-frequency-thresholded support. Expected diversity contraction does not imply that every realized D_t decreases. Tests must include a possible transition from p=(0.9,0.1) to child frequencies (0.5,0.5), where realized diversity increases.

### 4.3 Null SHL and variable-size qualification

**Source-derived: SI section 5.4, p.12, and section 8.3, p.19.** For n>1:

\[
\operatorname{SHL}_0(n)=\frac{\ln 2}{-\ln(1-1/n)}.
\]

Use the separate name `structural_half_life_null`, unit `categorical_recursive_rounds`, and an explicit null-scenario identity. Numerically evaluate the denominator stably, for example through the mathematically equivalent `-log1p(-1/n)`, without altering its definition.

The formula supplies a continuous timescale. An optional integer first-halving step is a separately named ceiling of that timescale. Neither is automatically the first zero-support time of a realized path. Positive D_0 is needed to interpret an actual expected-diversity halving. With D_0=0, the parameter formula may be displayed as a conditional timescale, but `halving_applicability` must state that no positive diversity is being halved.

n=1 is outside the logarithmic formula's domain. Its one-step collapse of a positive-diversity categorical draw may be described separately; do not report SHL=0, silently set n=2, or use infinity in JSON. n<=0, booleans and nonintegers fail validation. No n is inferred from token count, checkpoint size, neural training rows, or probe count.

**Engineering extension, explicitly conditional:** For a declared deterministic schedule n_0,...,n_(t-1) of otherwise identical closed transitions, the tower-property calculation gives

\[
\mathbb E[D_t]=D_0\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}(1-1/n_j).
\]

The schedule is exposed and used directly. A mean sample size cannot replace it. A first expected half-crossing in this schedule is named `null_schedule_first_half_crossing`; it is not the source's fixed-n SHL_0. Adaptive, state-dependent schedules do not use this product without an additional model and remain outside this implementation.

### 4.4 Generalized transition accounting

**Source-derived: SI section 6, pp.14-15.** The paper separates candidate mixture, selection, dataset assembly, model update and later output:

\[
u_t=(1-\lambda_t)p_t+\lambda_t e_t,\qquad
\widetilde u_{t,z}=\frac{w_{t,z}u_{t,z}}{\sum_jw_{t,j}u_{t,j}},
\]

followed by the recorded learning/update process and a new model-output distribution.

The implementation may compute u and the normalized selected distribution when all operands, class meanings and weight units are supplied. It must not implement an invented closed-form map from selected data to the next neural model distribution. All-zero selection mass makes the normalized selection undefined. Negative or incompatible weights fail; unknown weights stay unknown.

Archive size, token share and a field labeled `real_data_ratio` cannot automatically become the effective structural pre-selection weight lambda. Directly supplied lambda remains a declared model/scenario parameter unless independently supported by the relevant process records.

A source's entry, preservation through preprocessing, survival through selection at usable weight, actual training exposure, model update, and later expression are separately recorded. This supports SI's exogamy accounting without inventing an overall exogamy score. Formal or synthetic material can have independently grounded structure; human material can be derivative. Nominal provenance is never sufficient by itself.

## 5. Empirical Structural Half-Life and longitudinal sensitivity

### 5.1 Measurement target and declared fitting request

**Source-derived:** SI section 8.3 models an approximately geometric regime as

\[
D_t\approx D_0\,2^{-t/\operatorname{SHL}_{\mathrm{emp}}}.
\]

**Engineering specification submitted here:** `anchored_log_decay_v1` estimates a conditional descriptive decay timescale from compatible observed Gini-Simpson values. It is not a likelihood model of neural training, and it does not estimate SHL from Shannon entropy, observed class count, validity, or lexical distance.

A request declares the trajectory/path, view, exact start and end state IDs, actual recursive-round coordinates, expected measurement schedule, recurring panel set, aggregation rule, positive starting value, and a pre-inspection maximum absolute log residual. It also declares the uncertainty/sensitivity profile or explicitly records that uncertainty is not estimated.

No automatic search for a favorable start, a steeper segment, a convenient breakpoint, or an apparently geometric suffix is permitted. A revised exploratory interval gets its own registered request and exploratory status; it cannot replace the original result.

### 5.2 Local interval summaries

For a requested compatible pair with known recursive distance delta_t>0 and 0<D_b<D_a:

\[
\operatorname{SHL}_{a,b}=\frac{\Delta t\ln 2}{\ln(D_a/D_b)}.
\]

This is a log-rate summary over those two endpoints. Name it `structural_half_life_interval`, retain both endpoint values and the elapsed rounds, and do not claim a geometric path between them. An intentionally unsampled interval is shown as such; an unknown intervening process blocks its recursive-rate interpretation.

| Situation | Required result |
|---|---|
| Positive decline with valid endpoints and distance | Available interval timescale, exact endpoints, method and scope. |
| Equal positive diversity | Undefined declining half-life; `no_decay_on_interval`; display zero change separately. |
| Diversity increase | Undefined declining half-life; `growth_on_interval`; retain the increase. |
| Zero starting diversity | Undefined halving interpretation; no pseudocount. |
| Positive start and zero end | Undefined logarithmic endpoint timescale; preserve first observed zero and its bracketing measurements. Do not manufacture zero or a finite half-life. |
| Missing state, incomparable frame, unsupported distance or unknown endpoint | Unavailable with scoped reasons. |
| Request absent | Not requested. |

An observed bracket where D first falls to at most half its baseline is a separate `observed_half_crossing_bracket`. It records measured endpoints, not an unobserved exact crossing time or fitted SHL.

### 5.3 Anchored fit

For at least four registered measured states, including the anchor, all D_i>0, put

\[
x_i=t_i-t_0,\qquad y_i=\ln(D_i/D_0),\qquad
\widehat\beta=\frac{\sum_{i>0}x_i y_i}{\sum_{i>0}x_i^2}.
\]

The anchor is fixed; no freely fitted intercept changes D_0. Each registered measurement time has equal fitting weight. Report x_i, D_i, the anchor, slope, fitted values, residuals, maximum absolute log residual, and the requested fit interval.

When beta<0 and every prerequisite plus the predeclared residual check passes:

\[
\operatorname{SHL}_{\mathrm{emp}}=-\ln 2/\widehat\beta.
\]

The four-state minimum, equal-time weighting and residual rule are engineering choices. The papers supply no universal fit threshold. `max_abs_log_residual` must therefore be a finite positive prespecified request value, recorded with its rationale. There is no silent default R-squared cutoff or hard-coded claim of an adequate geometric regime. Failure to supply a regime criterion blocks the qualified global fit; local requested endpoint summaries may remain available.

Small local increases do not automatically invalidate an otherwise compatible noisy trajectory. The fit and residual criteria decide the registered geometric summary. A nonnegative slope gives no finite declining half-life. Non-geometric data preserve their values and applicable interval summaries instead of being forced into one global rate.

A zero, a missing expected measurement, an undocumented transition, a material protocol change, an intervening recovery treatment inside a supposed unchanged regime, or an incompatible recut blocks that particular fit. A newly declared post-intervention interval is separately analyzed, with a new anchor and scope.

### 5.4 Aggregation and replication units

Never pool all generated rows across states or branches. Per-cell diversity is computed first. A declared multi-panel curve uses the same prespecified panel set at every measured time, equal panel weights, and the mean of panel D values. It must be named `mean_panel_diversity`; it is not diversity of pooled class counts. No equal-size illusion is created by filling missing panels.

Independent lineages are different from repeated prompts. A root-level curve first forms the prespecified within-root panel mean, then uses equal root-unit weighting. Known shared stochastic ancestry, reused draws or common evolving history cannot supply independent root replicates merely through separate branch names. A shared fixed starting checkpoint or common reference alone does not prove that later independently randomized runs are statistically dependent. Conditional sampling independence and independent evidential origin are separately assessed, as required by EI.

The registered root unit is the actual stochastic replication/dependency unit with its common-parent conditioning visible. One predeclared path per dependent unit is used in the initial quantitative profile; other branches remain in the ledger and can be separately described. Unknown dependence cannot be resolved by an ID change. General multi-parent causal attribution and weighted branch-pooling estimators remain outside this slice.

Matched observed-probe sample budgets and full primary class coverage reduce one obvious sample-size confound but do not eliminate finite-sample bias or parameter-estimation uncertainty. The report must expose those limits. Corpus-stage fits remain labeled corpus-stage fits and cannot become model-capacity timescales.

### 5.5 Optional registered sensitivity procedure

`longitudinal_trajectory_sensitivity_v1` is a new engineering profile. It reuses the principle of whole-unit paired resampling, not Phase 2's six-block experiment identity. The request chooses one of two units in advance:

| Unit | What moves together | Permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `lineage_root` | The entire chosen trajectory and its fixed recurring panels for one recorded root/dependency unit. | Sensitivity across the supplied lineage units, conditional on the declared sampling and dependency model. No population guarantee from nominal root labels. |
| `probe_panel` | One recurring prompt/task panel across every measured state on one trajectory. | Fixed-panel sensitivity within that particular path. It does not quantify training-lineage variability. |

Require at least two eligible units, a complete common time grid, known grouping, the fixed fitting request, and no hidden unit substitution. Unknown independence restricts stronger inferences; a known shared-root dependency cannot be relabeled independent. If the grouping prerequisites cannot be established, the sensitivity interval is unavailable while separately supported descriptive point summaries remain possible.

Use 2,000 resamples, seed 20260919, an identified private RNG, a separately versioned plan ID, and stored full integer indices. This count and seed are engineering choices for reproducibility, not source prescriptions. Resample whole units, recompute the time curve and the registered fit for each draw, and retain cross-time dependence. Never bootstrap separate timepoints or single candidate strings as independent generations.

Use the fixed linear percentile interpolation at 0.025 and 0.975. Preserve every draw, its fit status and any failure reason. Do not delete non-declining, zero-diversity, missing or non-geometric draws before computing an apparently finite SHL interval. If any required draw lacks a finite applicable fit, the complete interval is unavailable; report the failure counts, point fit and any separate diagnostic distribution without a filtered-success interval.

Intervals are explicitly `resampling_sensitivity`, not automatically confidence intervals over all prompts, checkpoints or training pipelines. A single curve with no applicable resampling unit can still support a qualified descriptive local/point estimate, with `uncertainty_status: not_estimated`; it cannot claim a validated rate comparison. Null versus empirical timescales are displayed separately. A shorter empirical point timescale alone does not identify filtering, reward, selection, or another cause, or prove a statistically established acceleration.

## 6. Finite-family support assays before ERR

### 6.1 Why a separate assay is required

**Source-derived: SD section 5; SI sections 3.3 and 8.1; MET sections 13.1/13.3.** Finite nonappearance is not absence from expressible support. A class appearing once does not establish its generation probability above a chosen threshold. The first two phases did not implement the required finite-data estimator.

This phase therefore implements an explicit bounded assay before allowing a numeric evidence-qualified ERR. It does not replace expressible support with observed-set overlap.

An assay declares a finite admissible intervention family I_0, its version and complete member list, the fixed model/system state, task/horizon/resolution, reference registry, success event, trial design, prespecified counts, `min_generation_probability` tau, and error budget alpha. The target is support within **that family**, not every possible prompt, search algorithm, tool configuration or future intervention.

The `observed_intervention_union_size` separately reports the finite union of actually observed accepted classes under tested interventions. It is useful even when probability-threshold membership remains unresolved, but never inherits an expressible-support label.

### 6.2 Trial and success-event definition

The first built-in assay accepts independent fixed-design calls, one preselected candidate position per call, with the same registered intervention and fixed state within a cell. It can use a recorded multi-output call only if one position was chosen in advance for the assay. It cannot count five correlated candidates from that call as five independent Bernoulli trials.

The Bernoulli event is explicit:

- `class_membership`: the selected output instantiates class z, regardless of its separately recorded functional validity.
- `valid_class_expression`: the selected output instantiates z and satisfies the registered validity rubric.

These events get different assay identities and cannot be interchanged across pre/post stages. No valid-only or successfully classified-only denominator is used for the unconditional generation event.

For each intervention, record the planned fixed N and the actual attempted/completed eligible trial inventory. Optional stopping, adaptive targeting, changing model state, reused continuations, unresolved call dependence, and incomplete scheduled trials restrict the built-in binomial interpretation. They can remain descriptive observations; they are not repaired by increasing N or counting missing slots as failures.

A refusal or no-output completion can be a definite failure to express a class only when the registered event and original call record support that determination. Harness loss, unperformed calls, unreadable evidence and classification uncertainty do not automatically become negative outcomes. An unperformed trial prevents the fixed-N assay from completing.

Among N validly inventoried trials, retain `k_confirmed` positive events and `k_possible`, including unresolved events that could still be positive. Thus k_confirmed <= true successes <= k_possible <= N. This interval retains unresolved judgments without adding invented labels or deleting difficult trials.

### 6.3 Conservative probability-threshold procedure

**Engineering proposal:** `finite_family_binomial_threshold_v1` uses exact binomial tail tests against the fixed tau. It supplies a conservative simultaneous decision procedure conditional on the sampling and labeling assumptions.

Before data inspection, register the complete set of relevant `(stage, class, intervention)` cells. Let J be its size across the entire requested pre/post episode, including planned but later missing cells. For alpha in (0,1), use per-tail allocation

\[
\delta=\frac{\alpha}{2J}.
\]

Default alpha is 0.05 and must be explicitly materialized in the assay record before use. Tau is a required exact value in (0,1]; it has no default and is distinct from `min_empirical_frequency`.

For B distributed as Binomial(N,tau), compute exactly:

\[
T_+(k)=\Pr(B\ge k),\qquad T_-(k)=\Pr(B\le k).
\]

For an eligible intervention cell:

- `threshold_present` when T_+(k_confirmed) <= delta. This is the inclusive lower-bound-at-tau rule.
- `threshold_below` when T_-(k_possible) < delta. The strict inequality is required to establish an upper bound **below** tau.
- Otherwise `unresolved`.

Malformed design, inconsistent trial identity, incompatible state, or unmet sampling assumptions produce `unavailable`, not below-threshold evidence. Missing evidence about those assumptions remains explicit and prevents an unqualified probability claim. Exact rational comparisons are required; a display rounding tolerance cannot change equality at the decision boundary.

Across the finite family, a class is `threshold_present` if at least one eligible intervention establishes it. It is `threshold_below` only if every preregistered intervention establishes below-threshold status. Otherwise it is unresolved or unavailable with the underlying reasons. An untested intervention cannot be dropped to make the universal below-threshold condition easier.

The two-tail allocation bounds the family of elementary errors by alpha under the stated assumptions, by binomial monotonicity and a union bound. Independence between different classes or between tests is not required for that union bound. The independence/stationarity requirements within each trial cell still matter. Appendix C gives the derivation and exact boundary examples. This statistical argument does not validate imported labels, actual model stability, or independent origin.

### 6.4 Interpretation, uncertainty and input limits

Reports retain N, both success bounds, tau, alpha, J, delta, both exact tails, all intervention statuses, missing trials, model-state identity, support event and evidence qualifications. They report `finite_family_scope` prominently.

`expressible_support_size_finite_family` is an evidence-qualified count of the registered reference classes meeting the criterion within I_0, only when the required registry class statuses are resolved and the corresponding substantive assumptions have their recorded basis. Its mandatory `class_universe_ref` and `coverage_scope: registered_reference_classes` prevent it from claiming an exhaustive count of every valid mechanism outside the represented cut. With unresolved classes, report a known-present set and unresolved set plus set-size bounds, not an exact total. A descriptive union remains separately calculable. Registry normalization uses this expressible set only; an empty registry gives undefined normalization.

A resolved support or missing set is resolved under the named finite-family statistical decision and supplied evidence, not proven equal to the model's unknowable unrestricted repertoire. Below-threshold probability can still be positive and can still yield occasional observations; it is not an absorbing zero or a representational-extinction claim. Exact set arithmetic describes the resolved assay sets, with their statistical error budget retained.

Record-shaped evidence can be checked by software but is not independently certified by the toolkit. Real-import conclusions are explicitly conditional on supplied qualified evidence. Fixture truth declarations exercise arithmetic and failure paths without establishing the corresponding real-world facts.

Counts and tail calculations must satisfy declared arithmetic limits before evaluation. Resource failure returns scoped unavailability; it cannot trigger a normal approximation, a smaller sample, or a different alpha. Tau=1 is legal but finite successes generally cannot establish probability exactly one by this assay. That limitation is reported rather than evaded.

## 7. External Recovery Rate, exogamy, and measurement reopening

### 7.1 Source quantity and qualification gates

**Source-derived: SI section 8.4, p.19.** Let the pre-intervention missing reference set be

\[
\mathcal M=\mathcal R\setminus S_{\mathrm{expr}}^{\mathrm{pre}},
\qquad
\operatorname{ERR}=\frac{|\mathcal M\cap S_{\mathrm{expr}}^{\mathrm{post}}|}{|\mathcal M|}.
\]

Recovery requires an independently grounded intervention, external validation, and changed ability to express the missing structure. Directly pasting the target answer into the output does not qualify.

The implemented field remains `external_recovery_rate`, with a mandatory finite-family/success-event qualification and a versioned assay method. It is the unweighted quantity; no severity weights are introduced in this phase.

The same relevant task, horizon, resolution, registry, threshold, success event, and assay interpretation must apply pre/post. The model's parameters may change during a recovery intervention, but each assay stage holds its own state fixed. Record separately `changes_model_parameters`, `changes_tools`, `changes_retrieval`, `changes_conditioning`, and the resulting state identities.

Adding tools or grounded retrieval is permitted by SI. The pre/post assay tasks remain a common registered evaluation family applied to the explicitly changed system configuration. An intervention unavailable before the treatment can be represented by its prespecified pre-treatment system behavior. It cannot simply be removed from the pre family or newly selected after observing the post results. A materially revised testing family requires a new paired design; it does not silently replace the original missing-set criterion.

### 7.2 Construct the missing set without treating uncertainty as absence

The first implemented automatic set-construction path uses section 6's finite-family assay. A known pre set requires each reference class to be established as present or below threshold within the fixed family. Pre-unresolved classes do not enter M as missing.

For a known M, every member's post status must resolve before an exact evidence-qualified ERR is reported. Post-unresolved classes cannot be assigned a zero recovery outcome merely because they were not observed in one batch.

| State of evidence | Main ERR result and retained information |
|---|---|
| Known nonempty M; all its post statuses resolved; intervention and validation gates satisfied | Available exact numerator/denominator and finite-family-qualified fraction. |
| Known empty M | Undefined, `empty_missing_reference_set`; no 0 or 1. |
| Pre missing set unresolved | Unavailable; show known-present, known-below and unresolved pre classes, with no invented denominator. |
| Known M; some post statuses unresolved | Main ERR unavailable; optionally show separate deterministic partial-information bounds `[confirmed_recovered/card(M), (confirmed_recovered + unresolved_post)/card(M)]`. These are not a point ERR or a confidence interval. |
| Independent grounding, recovery validation, or non-injection requirement unresolved/failed | Evidence-qualified ERR unavailable; preserve descriptive pre/post sets and the failing gate. |
| Task/threshold/view/registry meaning changed incompatibly | Unavailable comparison; retain each separately scoped assay. |
| ERR not requested | Not requested. |

A separately supplied externally reviewed assay may be retained as evidence, but arbitrary `expressible: true/false` labels cannot bypass the built-in record and criterion checks. Supporting a second estimator requires a later versioned contract. Exact categorical toy sets may be used as stipulated fixtures/scenarios and must remain labeled as such.

### 7.3 Evidence needed for a recovery episode

Each episode identifies the registered hypothesis and evaluation scope, pre/post snapshots, finite assay family, missing-set decision, intervention source/knowledge type/transformation lineage, intervention material, allowed exposure, model/configuration change, qualified independent review, non-injection assessment, and post-intervention expression checks.

All indispensable evidence paths preserve mock/fixture, unknown, unreadable, conflicting, withdrawn, or post-hoc states. A nonmock parent cannot promote a mock child into actual independent evidence. Different providers or separate interface names do not establish exogamy.

The output records separate evidence for entry, preprocessing fidelity, selection survival, actual update/exposure, and later expression. It does not infer these stages from a before/after score. An intervention may supply a missing class through tools at inference without updating parameters; its claim is assisted system expression under those conditions. A training intervention identifies a new model state and cannot be described as recovery solely from the old model's latent representation.

Direct answer substitution is excluded. Relevant worked solutions in legitimate training material do not automatically invalidate a learning intervention; the post-intervention evidence must demonstrate expression under the preregistered evaluation rather than merely paste the supplied target into the scored output. Exact permitted training/retrieval exposure and distinct evaluation materials remain reviewable. Legitimate task evidence, simulator constraints, tool results and retrieved material must be assessed under the registered recovery protocol. The toolkit checks an evidence-qualified review record and artifact links; it does not pretend to decide independence or answer leakage perfectly from natural-language text. Unknown or disputed non-injection status withholds the qualified claim.

No automatic causal claim of superiority over self-resampling is produced. Such an SI section 10.3 comparison would additionally require matched treatment/control budgets, assignment and analysis rules, and real independent evidence.

### 7.4 Distributional reopening, structural reopening, and schema change

EC's two reopening notions retain their own records. `distributional_reopening_event` adds reality-bearing cases within an existing represented state space. `structural_reopening_event` records an evidenced change to the cut. A receipt, timestamp, new file name, or unexplained new label establishes neither.

The first profile computes exact longitudinal deltas and ERR only within a common supported schema. A split/merge/recut event preserves old/new identities, original anomaly artifacts, reasons, reviewers and affected results. It interrupts direct numerical comparability. Do not sum aggregate counts into a new ontology or count the same old outputs as new model samples.

To analyze both endpoints under a revised supported cut, all relevant observations need new evidence-qualified assignments under that cut. Until that capability and evidence are supplied, the cross-cut comparison remains unavailable; unaffected within-cut segments remain valid. This phase records arbitrary proposed schema revisions but does not expand the production sorting classifier into an automatic arbitrary-ontology engine.

A class-count increase caused by reannotation is a measurement revision. It cannot count as recovered model support, effective external training presence, or a positive ERR episode. An anomaly can remain unresolved and valuable without receiving counted class mass.

## 12. Security, reproducibility, and evidence limits

### 12.1 Security boundary

All model outputs, prompts, evidence, lineage descriptions, external URLs, correction notes and replay files are passive data. They cannot select imports, execute an expression, run a shell, install a dependency, invoke a network service, alter a permissions policy or authorize a different plan step.

Reuse the existing safe local reader and atomic nonreplacement report transaction. The new formulas add denial-of-service risks through graph expansion, combinatorial enumeration, exact fractions and large exponents; resource checks are part of required conformance. Production code does no unbounded Cartesian enumeration of categorical outcomes. Such enumeration is restricted to tiny trusted test oracles.

Test private roots, raw bodies, identity mappings and arbitrary text across both reports and CLI errors. A hash is not a substitute for access-controlled evidence and should not be described as anonymization. Stored indices are inert integers, not executable transformations.

The recorded predecessor environment is CPython 3.13.5 / Linux with Unicode 15.1.0 and no third-party site loading. Continue to record the actual environment and fail safely when required secure platform operations are unavailable. No native Windows/macOS, hostile-OS sandbox, network-filesystem or universal durability assurance is added merely by this plan. Build/install/hosted CI testing remains separately authorized work.

### 12.2 Reproducibility boundary

Every new result is bound to the actually consumed input, configuration, method and software identities. A fixed run context produces identical canonical report values and replay results. Declared recording timestamps and runtime/platform metadata are separately identified fields. Code changes may legitimately change implementation fingerprints and run IDs; they do not justify changing old numerical oracles or overwriting historical reports.

Archive validation must check ZIP integrity, exact relative paths, every project file's content identity, a clean extraction, the full current suite, and CLI replay. Record a Git tree separately from a SHA-256 file manifest. A locally computed Git object hash is not proof that GitHub accepted or published that object.

Large objects and tool limits are delivery issues. Preserve complete matrices, logs and input data. Do not shorten files, regenerate different fixtures or split an exact byte identity merely to make an upload appear successful. Use an authorized alternative transfer path when necessary, then read back the complete intended tree. If delivery still fails, provide exact local files and keep publication pending.

### 12.3 What this phase cannot establish through fixtures

Fixtures and software checks do not provide real independent human annotations, verified model stability, successful program execution, genuine external-data independence, a validated mechanism ontology, actual training exposure, or a recovered ability. These are substantive E duties. They require evidence of the corresponding type, not another synthetic statement that the duty was fulfilled.

The Phase 3 instrument can correctly report a conditional estimate, a mathematical undefined result, or an unresolved scientific question. Such behavior is part of conformance. UD-006 remains open for real studies; UD-007 remains deferred. Assay confidence calculations are conditional sampling statements and cannot certify the correctness of labels or erase lineage uncertainty.

The sources' predictions concerning earlier tail failure, external correction, shared-model ancestry and delayed fluency decline remain research questions to evaluate with properly designed real studies. This phase supplies measurements and records for those studies. It does not manufacture a universal favorable empirical verdict.

## Appendix C. Derivation of the finite-family threshold decision

This appendix supplies reasoning for the proposed assay rather than attributing it to SI or SD.

For a fixed eligible intervention cell, let the unobserved complete true success count K have Binomial(N,p) distribution under the registered fixed-state independent-trial assumptions. Let k_L be the confirmed success count and k_U the maximum count still consistent with unresolved outcomes. The record rules ensure k_L <= K <= k_U without conditioning the denominator on observed successes.

For a claim that p is at least tau, select a count cutoff whose binomial upper tail at tau is at most delta. For every p<tau, monotonicity of that tail bounds the probability of crossing the cutoff by delta. Using k_L instead of K can only make that crossing harder, so unresolved classifications cannot increase the false-positive risk under this rule.

For a claim that p is below tau, select a count cutoff whose binomial lower tail at tau is strictly less than delta. For every p>=tau, monotonicity bounds the probability of crossing the low-count cutoff by delta. Using k_U instead of K can only make that crossing harder. The strict comparison preserves the inclusive meaning of the supported class set at p=tau.

There are at most 2J elementary tail statements in the preregistered episode. Allocating delta=alpha/(2J) and applying a union bound gives an error bound at most alpha under the stated assumptions. No independence between those statements is needed. The result does require the correct fixed-N sampling model and valid k bounds. A false label, an unperformed call entered as a failure, selective trial omission, unsupported independence, adaptive stopping, or changed state breaks that interpretation; a complete software form cannot repair it.

Exact examples for the boundary tests:

- At N=4 and tau=1/2, P(B>=4)=1/16 and P(B<=0)=1/16. With delta=1/16, the inclusive positive rule accepts four confirmed successes, while the strict below rule does not accept zero possible successes at equality.
- At N=4 and tau=3/4, P(B<=0)=(1/4)^4=1/256. With delta=1/16, zero possible successes supports below-threshold status. An unresolved outcome may increase k_U and change that decision.

These examples specify unit-test arithmetic; they are not the default alpha or multiplicity configuration of a real study. Report the actual J and delta rather than borrowing these demonstration values.
